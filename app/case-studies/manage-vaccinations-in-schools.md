We delivered a simple, easy-to-use, robust, end-to-end service – Managing Vaccination in Schools (Mavis) – that meets the needs of multiple stakeholders and users.

Mavis helps school age immunisation service (SAIS) teams manage their vaccination programmes end-to-end, removing the problems of system fragmentation and manual data entry, as well as improving data quality and vaccine uptake.

Our work shows how a user-centered, iterative, and clinically rigorous approach can rapidly transform an essential service and shape national policy.

- **2.17 million vaccinations** administered during the first 12 months

- **235% increase** in responses from parents giving consent

- **£15 million** predicted annual savings
  {.c-box-list}

## The end-to-end, test, learn and grow approach

The NHS came to us with multiple policy objectives covering a complex landscape of childhood vaccinations: disparate providers, incompatible systems, and unmatched data sources.

Our discovery work started broad: we mapped the entire ecosystem, and identified how, when and where vital vaccination data and information gets lost in the ‘Bermuda triangle’ between GPs, SAIS teams, and child health services.

However, instead of trying to fix the whole system, we adopted a deliberate test, learn, and grow approach, where we would start by solving one of the many problems we’d identified, then learning what and where to scale most effectively.

We chose school-age vaccinations as our starting point: an area with clear pain points, a few moving parts, and no existing service that solved the whole problem end-to-end. By building Mavis as the first fully integrated service for this slice of the system, we demonstrated how an end-to-end service solves a pressing problem.

Starting with one vaccination type in a limited area provided valuable real-world feedback for rapid iteration, helping us learn what works, and then rapidly building upon it, enabling us to deliver a comprehensive, end-to-end service that allows:

- Matching and consolidation of child records from multiple sources.
- SAIS teams to manage child cohorts for multiple vaccination types.
- Parents/carers to automatically receive and respond to online consent requests.
- SAIS teams to triage children and record vaccinations.
- Vaccination data to be automatically shared with NHS and GP systems.

## Meeting the needs of the NHS

The Mavis program was explicitly shaped by NHS stakeholder demands for:

- More timely, higher-quality data for planning and monitoring.
- A single, unified data entry point.
- Better mechanisms for responding to outbreaks and offering routine vaccinations outside of GP settings.

Our shared goal was simple: Vaccinate more children more easily.

We addressed known problems – low uptake, outdated consent, and fragmented data – to design a solution that provides those that need it with easy access to accurate, timely vaccination data, fulfilling core NHS requirements.

## Daily release in a highly regulated environment

To gain the essential feedback we needed to learn about and grow the service, it was vital that we maintained a rapid development pace, with small, frequent, often daily releases.

Working in a highly regulated clinical environment, adhering to the most stringent standards, traditionally assured through lengthy governance processes and onerous documentation, typically prevents such rapid iteration.

However, by collaborating with numerous data protection, clinical safety, solutions assurance and service management colleagues and governance forums across the NHS, we demonstrated that daily software releases are possible while ensuring clinical safety and regulatory compliance. We changed how the NHS assures the clinical safety of software.

> It’s quite humbling the lengths those engineers went to, just so that ordinary road users – men and women that they would never meet – could avoid going into Birmingham. And when I think about that, I actually get quite chocked up.

## Informing NHS policy

Mavis was engineered not just as a delivery mechanism but as an engine for evidence-based policy. By building the service quickly and getting it into the hands of users as soon as possible, we gained real data on vaccine uptake and service performance. Data that would have taken years to obtain had we not demonstrated the value of starting small.

Mavis has therefore become a validation point for national strategies to offer more childhood vaccinations in more settings, and to use the NHS App to signpost missed vaccinations. Mavis is poised to consolidate information from multiple sources, providing foundational data that directly informs and improves NHS policy for public health outcomes.
